# Branding — FilterFlow

## Name history

- Started as "Madden's Automations" — family feedback: sounds unprofessional/personal-side-hustle rather than a real business.
- Explored single-word, Squarespace-style names (Cornerstone, Groundwork, Homebase, etc.)
- Landed on **FilterFlow**: "Filter" = filtering out busywork, "Flow" = smooth/efficient operations as a result. Chosen for being easy to say aloud, memorable, and meaningfully tied to the actual service (not just a nice sound).

## Trademark check (done, both clear)

- **US (USPTO)**: "FilterFlow" and "Filter Flow" exist as registered trademarks, but only in unrelated industries — medical IV filters (B. Braun Medical Inc.) and textile knitting machine parts. Nothing in business/software services (the relevant category).
- **Canada (CIPO)**: searched directly, zero results for "FilterFlow." Closest unrelated hit was "FILTERCORP" (air/water filtration equipment).
- Conclusion (not legal advice, but reasonably thorough non-lawyer due diligence): safe to proceed for a small local business at current scale. If the business ever scales significantly (real staff, national reach), worth a real lawyer-conducted search at that point — not urgent now.
- Also found a near-dormant Instagram account under "FilterFlow" (1 follower, 1 post) — judged extremely low real-world risk.

## Website platform decision

- **Old**: Carrd — used for Joe's Plumbing demo and first portfolio draft. Real reliability issues encountered: edits not saving/publishing consistently, browser tab sync problems, blue focus-ring styling issues on embedded forms.
- **New**: **Wix** — chosen after comparing against Framer, Squarespace, Canva's website builder.
  - Wix: AI generates a first draft from a conversational Q&A, then hands over a full Canva-like drag-and-drop editor. Good balance of AI-assist + free editing control. Also has "Wix Studio" for more advanced/client-facing work later, so it scales into real client website-building without needing to switch platforms again.
  - Framer: more powerful/precise visual design (Figma-like canvas), but steeper learning curve. Decided NOT to learn preemptively — only pick up later if a specific future client's needs call for high-end custom design (e.g. a design agency, photographer).
  - Canva's website builder: considered but redundant with Wix; Canva is being used only for logo/graphic design, not as a website builder.
- **Important limitation noted**: Claude does NOT have a direct backend connector to Wix/Framer the way it does to Make.com. Troubleshooting there works the same screenshot-based way as Carrd did — slower than the Make.com debugging experience.

*(Update from later conversation: this later shifted again — decided to move to Claude Code for the real build instead of Wix, specifically for real file-level control. See file 06 for details.)*

## Logo direction

- Concept: blue-based, a swirl (representing the automation/cycling process) transitioning into a flowing wave line leading into the word "Flow" — visually tells the actual "filter → smooth flow" story, not just decorative.
- Font direction: soft/rounded, slightly flowing, still professional — landed on **Quicksand** as the top pick (also liked: Poppins Light, Comfortaa, Nunito Light).
- Built in Canva (free tier), using existing "wave logo" / "swirl logo" templates as a base rather than building from scratch.
- **Color feedback**: all-blue was flagged as not eye-catching enough — blue alone tends to recede rather than pop, especially on a white background.
  - Fix in progress: keep blue as the primary/trust color, add ONE accent color for contrast. Leading option discussed: keep "Filter" in dark navy, make "Flow" a warm accent (orange suggested as the classic high-contrast pairing with blue) instead of light blue.
- Also flagged: avoid the "glossy/gradient AI-generated" look (heavy shine, gradient fills) — flatter, simpler design reads as more genuinely professional for a small local-facing service brand.
- Status: logo not fully finalized as of last update — color contrast fix was the next step.

*(Update from later conversation: iterated further on the logo concept in a Claude Artifact — kept the spiral-into-wave idea but redesigned as a single continuous tapering line, flat solid colors only, navy #132C5C + teal #12B3A0 + one small coral/orange accent at the spiral-to-wave transition point. Wordmark: "Filter" in navy, "Flow" in teal, Quicksand bold. Considered switching the accent from orange to coral #E74D5A to avoid a "looks like Walmart" feel.)*

## Target audience note (relevant to all branding decisions)

Primary audience: older/middle-aged local business owners who are NOT tech-savvy and may find "AI" or "automation" intimidating or confusing as words. Branding should sound warm, plain-spoken, and trustworthy — avoid leaning on tech jargon in the name/tone itself, even though the actual service is AI-powered.
